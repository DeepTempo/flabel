# The flabel label corpus, as of 2026-08-28

**Read this before using the corpus as ground truth.** It exists because the review of P4-4 was
right that the numbers alone would let a consumer over-trust what is in the store, and because
`CLAUDE.md` puts label trustworthiness above everything else.

## What it is

24 captures, labelled into the `flabel` BigQuery dataset. `blfile` (bare, no flags) emits **880
flows**, every one carrying a recorded `gs://` origin.

| | flows | what it means |
| :-- | --: | :-- |
| tier 1 only | 400 | the NGFW's own threat log flagged it; Suricata did not |
| **both tiers** | **8** | both flagged the same flow, from two independent runs |
| tier 2 only | 472 | Suricata flagged it; see the caveat below |

## The corpus has two populations, and you must not mix them

- **17 captures carry tier 1 *and* tier 2.** For these, a flow with only a tier-2 source means the
  firewall looked and did not flag it.
- **7 captures carry tier 2 only** — they have never been replayed past the device. For these, the
  identical record means *nothing ever looked at tier 1*.

**So "no tier-1 label" is ambiguous across the corpus.** A model that learns "tier-1 absence is a
signal" would be learning which captures happened to be replayed in August. Distinguish them with
`runs[].capture_sha256` and `runs[].supplies` in the collection document, or with
`authoritative_runs` in the store. §6.4's own standard says recoverable-by-cross-referencing is
weaker than a field, and it is right; `coverage.tiers_examined` is the fix and it is not built yet.

## 54% of the corpus is unreviewed automated verdicts

The previous corpus was 100% device verdicts. This one is **472 of 880 (54%) tier-2-only**: Suricata
hits from snapshot `682c24ae30631504`, 83,964 rules, including wholesale-admitted sources —
`pawpatrules` among them, which `docs/prd.md` calls the broadest-scope and least-vetted admitted
source, with its false-positive risk knowingly accepted on the strength of the Goal 5 canary.

**The canary is two committed fixtures in CI. It does not look at these 24 captures and cannot.** A
measured false-positive rate is an explicit PRD non-goal. So the precision of the majority of this
corpus is argued from source provenance and ruleset curation, not measured.

| reproduction | the pre-operation baseline document rebuilds **identically** against the post-operation store |

## What is not

- **No false-positive rate.** Not measured, and a PRD non-goal.
- **Not a claim that unlabelled flows are benign.** `docs/prd.md`: flabel labels malicious flows; it
  is not an assertion of benign.
- **`coverage.unmatched_ratio` is null for the 17 two-tier captures**, because their tier-1 runs
  predate #84 and one missing count nulls the aggregate. Null means *not measured*, by design.
- **`flow.ja4` is null on flows whose struct comes from an August run** — ja4 was installed on
  2026-08-27. A third split between the two populations.

## Who may read this

**Deferred, explicitly, 2026-08-28.** `docs/spec-label-store.md` §9 lists "who may read the dataset"
as undecided, and `docs/PLAN-phase-4.md` asked for it to be settled or deferred *in writing* before
P4-4. It was not, and P4-4 ran — so this paragraph is the deferral being written down late rather
than pretended away.

Current state, unchanged by this phase: the dataset is readable by the instance service account and
by project members holding the default dataset ACL (#161 — project Editors are writers, which is
itself open). No new grant was made. The store now holds non-anonymous network metadata — DNS names,
HTTP URIs, TLS server names, internal addresses — for 24 real captures rather than for one, and the
archive holds a per-flow `eve.json` for each.

**Before any external consumer receives a `labels-collection`, or before
`roles/bigquery.dataViewer` is granted to anyone, that decision has to be made properly.**
